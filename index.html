Proffessional Background

I am a data analyst and researcher with a background in statistics, data analysis, and research. I currently work in a research and data-focused environment, where my work involves data collection and management, data cleaning, statistical analysis, research support, reporting, and translating data into meaningful insights for decision-making. My week typically involves working with tools such as R, Microsoft Excel, MySQL, Python, and other research and data-management platforms, while also developing my skills in data systems and teaching practical data analysis.

I believe statistics is a language for making better decisions under uncertainty — and that most people never got to understand it well.

By day, I work as a data analyst, cleaning messy datasets, building models, and turning ambiguous business questions into testable hypotheses and clear dashboards. By evening, I teach introductory and intermediate statistics, translating the same ideas — distributions, significance, regression — into language that actually sticks.

I have a strong background in statistics, data analysis, and research, with academic and practical experience in data management, statistical modelling, research methodology, and the use of analytical tools such as R, Microsoft Excel, SPSS, MySQL, and Python. Through working on diverse research and data-driven projects, I developed a strong interest in teaching, particularly in equipping learners with practical, industry-relevant analytical skills and empowering them to use data effectively for informed decision-making.

Outside of spreadsheets, I'm usually reading about data science, technology, business, and emerging trends, exploring new analytical tools and ideas, or keeping active through fitness and outdoor activities. I also enjoy reading, connecting with people, and following developments in Kenya and beyond—especially those shaping careers, innovation, and the future of work. And, of course, I firmly believe that a well-labelled axis is a form of kindness.
