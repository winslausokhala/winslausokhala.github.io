I automated my own job hunt.

A Gmail-based bot that scans job-alert emails, scores them against my CV, and drafts the applications — so I only spend time on the ones worth sending.

Job-alert emails pile up fast, and most of them aren't worth opening. I built a Python tool that reads every job-alert email in my Gmail and scores each listing against my CV — Data Analyst, Statistician, Junior Data Scientist, Research Assistant, Database/Data Management, and Graduate Trainee roles all count, and I tuned the matching to keep entry-level data-entry roles in while screening out off-topic listings, like finance-only "graduate trainee" postings.

It doesn't touch LinkedIn directly or auto-apply anywhere — that crosses into ToS territory I'd rather avoid. Instead, for every strong match it writes a tailored draft straight into my Gmail Drafts folder. I still read and send every application myself; the bot just does the sorting and the first pass at the cover letter.

It runs on its own schedule — a self-contained loop that rechecks every 60 minutes — and I built a small desktop app around it (Preview, Create Drafts, Auto Mode) so I never have to open a terminal to run it.
