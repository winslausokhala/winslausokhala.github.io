What are my actual odds, East Africa?

A structural analysis of labour demand for data-analytics skills across Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania and Rwanda — built from public labour-market, salary and GDP data, not vibes.

As a final-year Applied Statistics & IT student, I wanted a real answer to "how employable is my skill-set in this region" — grounded in numbers rather than the usual mix of optimism and anxiety. So I pulled together labour statistics, salary bands, and sector-growth data from KNBS, the World Bank, PayScale, and regional job boards, and built a structural read of the market instead of a guess.

There's no official dataset that computes a "probability of employment" for one named person — that needs microdata no public source publishes. What I built instead weighs the real, checkable indicators: how in-demand is data/analytics specifically (top-10 skill in Kenya for 2026), how fast is the sector growing relative to the wider economy (2.2x), and where the genuine headwinds sit (only 15% of new jobs are formal-sector; ~5-year average time-to-first-role for graduates nationally).

The short version: a quantitative, technical skill-set is measurably better positioned than the average graduate profile — but the real lever isn't the degree, it's demonstrable proof-of-work.
